Once you have a distribution you still have a problem: the long tail. Thousands
of tokens each hold a vanishing sliver of probability, and in aggregate that
sliver is large enough to get picked. Truncation throws the tail away before
sampling.

## Top-k

Keep the `k` highest-probability tokens, renormalise, sample. Simple and
predictable — you always have exactly `k` candidates.

That predictability is also the flaw. When the model is certain, `k=40` drags in
39 tokens it had already dismissed. When the model is genuinely torn between
sixty options, `k=40` cuts off twenty reasonable ones.

## Top-p (nucleus)

Keep the smallest set of tokens whose cumulative probability reaches `p`. The
candidate count now adapts: tight distributions yield few candidates, flat ones
yield many.

## The ordering trap

Temperature is applied **before** truncation. This is the detail that makes
shared configs meaningless:

| Temperature | Tokens kept at `p = 0.9` |
| ----------- | ------------------------ |
| 0.4         | ~3                       |
| 0.8         | ~8                       |
| 1.6         | ~31                      |

Same `p`, same logits, an order of magnitude apart. Reporting a top-p value
without the temperature beside it communicates nothing.

Turn the knob on the [temperature visualiser](/instruments/temperature-visualiser)
and watch the cut line move.
