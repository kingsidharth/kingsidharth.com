Everything so far was one token at a time, on a bench, with hand-picked
candidates. Here is the whole loop, recorded: one prompt sent to Gemma 4 E4B
three times, changing nothing but the sampling settings. The right answer is
153,136,992.

<SamplingCompare />

Read the greedy run first. Every partial product is correct — and then the
addition jams, the same broken sum repeats five times, and the model escapes
the loop by claiming it verified the result "using a calculator". It did not,
and the answer is wrong. This is the failure the cover of this guide warns
about: temperature 0 did not make the model careful, it made it stuck.

The middle run is hotter than most people would dare — temperature 1.0 with a
wide top-k, straight off the model card. It wanders, mis-multiplies, notices,
recomputes, and cross-checks itself with a second method. Both methods agree,
and both are right. Wandering is what self-correction looks like from the
outside.

The third run is what a broken sampler actually feels like: a nucleus cut so
tight the tail is gone, with two penalties punishing repetition on top. The
model can't even repeat its own intermediate results, the trace dissolves into
LaTeX noise, and the "answer" is not a number.

One prompt. The model was the same all three times. The sampler was the whole
difference.
