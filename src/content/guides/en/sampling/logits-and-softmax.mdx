A language model's final layer emits one real number per vocabulary entry. These
are logits. They are unbounded, they are not probabilities, and comparing two of
them across different prompts means nothing.

Softmax turns them into a distribution:

```python
def softmax(logits, temperature=1.0):
    scaled = [l / temperature for l in logits]
    m = max(scaled)                      # subtract the max for stability
    exps = [math.exp(s - m) for s in scaled]
    total = sum(exps)
    return [e / total for e in exps]
```

Two things worth noticing.

## Temperature divides, it does not add

Dividing every logit by `T` before exponentiating is what makes temperature
non-linear in its effect. `T < 1` widens the gaps between logits, so the top
token pulls further ahead. `T > 1` compresses them toward each other.

At `T → 0` the distribution collapses onto the argmax — that is greedy decoding,
and it is the same thing as `temperature=0`.

## The subtraction is not cosmetic

`exp()` of a large logit overflows a float. Subtracting the maximum before
exponentiating shifts everything into a safe range without changing the result,
because softmax is invariant to a constant shift. Every production implementation
does this; if you write your own and skip it, you will get `NaN` on long prompts
and blame the model.
