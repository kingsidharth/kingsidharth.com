A transformer is a stack of identical blocks — 12 in the original small ones,
96 in GPT-3, more in newer models. Understand one block and you understand
the architecture. Here is one, opened up:

One block does exactly two things, in order:

```
x = x + Attention(LayerNorm(x))     # positions talk to each other
x = x + MLP(LayerNorm(x))           # each position thinks alone
```

That `x +` in front of everything is the residual stream from the previous
chapter. Each sub-layer reads a normalised copy of the stream, computes, and
adds its result back. "Add & norm" in the diagrams is exactly this: the add
is the residual connection, the norm is LayerNorm keeping the numbers at a
scale the next stage can digest.

## The division of labour

**Attention is the only place tokens mix.** Everywhere else in the network,
each position's vector is processed independently — position 3 cannot see
position 7. Attention is the one communication channel: it moves information
between positions, weighted by relevance.

**The MLP is where positions process.** After attention has delivered
context to a position, the MLP transforms that position's vector — same MLP,
applied to every position separately, no cross-talk.

A useful rhythm to feel: attention gathers, MLP digests. Gather, digest,
gather, digest — once per block, dozens of times, each pass refining what's
written on the stream.

## Why the norm comes first

Modern transformers are "pre-norm": LayerNorm is applied to a *copy* of the
stream inside each sub-layer, while the stream itself flows through untouched
except for the additions. Post-norm (norm after the add, as in the 2017
paper) trains worse at depth. Pre-norm keeps a clean gradient highway from
the output back to the embedding, which is part of why hundred-layer models
converge at all.

## What a block costs

Roughly two-thirds of a block's parameters live in the MLP, one-third in
attention. When someone quotes a model's size, this is the unit being
counted: parameters per block, times blocks, plus the embedding tables at
either end.
